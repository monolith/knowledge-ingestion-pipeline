## 1. Statement classifier codebook: the fifteen label definitions

`create` · knowledge state **authoritative** · status `ready` · candidate `cand-001-r1` v2

**Slug** `statement-classifier-codebook-the-fifteen-label-definitions`

### Summary

The fifteen fine labels, each carried with the surface cue that fires it and the exclusions that stop it. These are the operative content of the codebook: the specification's own evidence is that definitions, not label count, determine whether the classifier works, so each is stated here in full rather than described.

### Assertions (14)

1. `observation` (coarse `case`, anchor κ 0.79) fires on an insight extrapolated from multiple events -- a pattern or reading drawn across more than one occasion, anecdotal by nature in that it holds so far on what has been seen, and not definitive enough to be a rule or a recommendation. A single occurrence reported as fact is an `event`; it becomes an `observation` when the sentence reads across occasions through a rate, a repetition, a sample, a trend or a mean.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

2. `event` (coarse `case`) fires on a single thing that happened, reported as fact: singular and factual. Quantity does not disqualify it -- an incident report full of counts and losses is still one occurrence. It excludes a reading drawn from what happened, which is an `observation`.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

3. `obligation` (coarse `method`) fires on what is owed to a third party: a requirement is an `obligation` only when there is someone outside the doing party to whom it is owed -- a regulator, an exchange, a counterparty, a client, a contract. It is the positive counterpart of `prohibition`, and excludes what must not be done and internal requirements.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

4. `prohibition` (coarse `method`) is the negative case of `procedure`: where a `procedure` says what to do, a `prohibition` says what must not be done. It is a hard rule, manual-grade -- it would be printed as an absolute and it can be violated. The test is whether someone could be in breach of it.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

5. `decision` (coarse `method`) fires on a choice reported as settled which governs what happens after it -- typically past-tense and agentive: adopted, chose, approved, standardised on, agreed to. It is the one `rule` label with no deontic modal to key on, which is why it needs its own surface cues.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

6. `procedure` (coarse `method`, anchor κ 0.74) fires on an established operational instruction -- how the system is used. The test is whether it could be pasted into a user manual unchanged and read as something to be followed every time. The discriminating question is whether the sentence reports practice or puts something forward; anything put forward is excluded.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

7. `recommendation` (coarse `method`) prescribes a course of action -- advice on what ought to be done, with no requirement force and nothing settled. It is practical and experiential, bearing on a choice where more than one valid option exists, and usually conversational in register. It excludes a requirement someone is accountable to.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

8. `definition` (coarse `concept`, anchor κ 0.81) fixes a key topic: it states what a term means and the term is load-bearing -- remove the definition and the reader cannot follow what follows. The grammatical centre is 'X is / means / refers to / is defined as Y'. It excludes a contingent statement that could turn out false.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

9. `distinction` (coarse `concept`) fires on a qualifier between multiple methods or options, resting on established differences rather than on one occasion's measurement. The contrast is the payload: two things are named and what separates them is the point. It excludes a single term being defined.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

10. `background` (coarse `concept`, anchor κ 0.87) fires on additional colour or history: it enriches understanding of the surround but does not define the topic itself. Strip it out and the topic is still defined; you have only lost context. Its excludes list is simply 'everything else', which is the point -- it is defined as the residual of the `concept` family.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

11. `principle` (coarse `model`) fires on the theory or rule behind something -- reasoning from fundamentals about why it holds or why it is built that way. First principles, not comparison, not measurement, and not implementation. The test is whether it would survive without the specific case in front of you.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

12. `architecture` (coarse `system`) fires on implementational design -- the construction of a physical or software system: what it is built out of, what components it has, what they are. It is not a logical scheme and not a configuration, and it excludes how the system is used.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

13. `assumption` (coarse `model`) fires on a leap of faith -- something taken on trust for the rest to hold, asserted without evidence and without proof, where the sentence knows it. Marker words are assumes, presupposes, conditional on, holds only if, provided that, on the standing assumption that. It excludes something needed to run rather than to be true.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

14. `dependency` (coarse `system`) fires on an established requirement -- a hard fact that the thing cannot run without. Marker words are requires, depends on, needs, is a prerequisite for, and 'without X you cannot'. It excludes something taken for granted for a claim to hold, which is an `assumption`.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

## 2. Statement classifier: taxonomy, contract and the boundaries between labels

`create` · knowledge state **authoritative** · status `ready` · candidate `cand-002-r1` v2

**Slug** `statement-classifier-taxonomy-contract-and-the-boundaries-between-labels`

### Summary

The two-tier taxonomy, the classifier's contract and scope, the pairwise separations that keep the labels apart, and the gaps the design leaves open.

### Assertions (10)

1. The taxonomy has five coarse types answering distinct questions -- `case` (what happened, on one occasion), `method` (what is done, required, forbidden, advised or settled), `concept` (what does this term mean), `model` (why does this hold, what does it rest on, how is it computed) and `system` (what is the thing built from, what does it need to run) -- plus `general`, which is assigned by code when no test fires.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

2. [VERIFIED] Within a single annotation scheme, per-category reliability varies by a factor of two, and the abstract categories are systematically worst: CoreSC measured Conclusion 0.89, Background 0.87, Object 0.81, Observation 0.79, Result 0.78 and Method 0.74, against Hypothesis 0.46, Motivation 0.46 and Model 0.43.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

3. [DESIGN] Merging `conclusion` into `finding` cuts against the other change in the same revision: `finding` already took 24% of all assignments and was behaving as a de-facto residual, so widening it increases that risk. The merged definition therefore tightens its exclusions against `fact` and `observation` rather than loosening them, and whether that holds is a re-test rather than a claim.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

4. The status ladder has four rungs, each adding a different thing: `floated` puts a point on the table, `proposed` adds specification, `evidenced` adds evidence, and `settled` adds certainty. The test separating the first two is whether you could act on the sentence as written or would first have to invent the parameters.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

5. [DESIGN] Provenance is a separate field and is not classified, because where a record came from is known at ingestion and costs no agreement -- it cannot be got wrong by a model that never infers it. It is distinct from status: status says how firmly a statement is held now, provenance says where it was born. Both are worth having; only one of them can be got wrong.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

6. [DESIGN] The reassurance marker routes to `recommendation` on the reasoning that procedures and principles do not have feelings: language that soothes, warns off or manages the reader's reaction -- don't panic, no need to worry, don't stress, ignore me -- marks the sentence as advice from a person rather than an operational instruction or a standing relation.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

7. Several separations are single discriminating questions: `observation`/`event` asks whether anything was measured; `event`/`decision` whether a choice was made; `principle`/`architecture` asks WHY it works versus HOW it is built, resolved by whether deleting the sentence would leave you unable to explain the idea or unable to rebuild the thing; `assumption`/`dependency` asks must-be-TRUE versus must-be-PRESENT; `formula`/`definition` asks whether it computes; and `definition`/`distinction` asks whether one term or two.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

8. The model answers one independent yes/no question per fine label in a single call and is never asked to pick one of fifteen. [VERIFIED] Multiclass framing measured 90% lower odds of correct detection than binary presence/absence framing (OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.03–0.35, p<.001, mixed-effects model over 1.1 million-plus annotations).

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

9. [DESIGN] An earlier revision had a `claim` coarse type whose three labels -- `fact`, `finding` and `proposition` -- differed mainly by how established a statement was, which contradicted the contract that the classifier does not judge truth. They were replaced by a single `status` field carried by every type: floated, proposed, evidenced, settled.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

10. [CAVEAT] The label renames are confounded with the pointer repair: through v2 the codebook's `observation` definition still pointed raters at `finding`, `study` and `conclusion` -- labels the restructure had removed -- and eleven such dangling pointers were found and repaired in the same revision as the renames. How much of the 37-pair collision was the missing label and how much was the broken codebook is not separated.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

## 3. What the statement classifier's in-house measurements found, including the rejected designs

`create` · knowledge state **supported** · status `ready` · candidate `cand-003-r1` v2

**Slug** `what-the-statement-classifier-s-in-house-measurements-found-including-the-reject`

### Summary

Twenty-two runs on this codebook, roughly 10,000 assignments, no answer key in existence. Includes the four designs that were tested and measured worse than what they replaced.

### Assertions (42)

1. The statement classifier's measured reliability is Krippendorff α 0.934 on 160 statements from eight generated sources and 0.894 on 85 statements from three published documents, from eight blind raters working the codebook verbatim with no answer key in existence. For scale, the argumentative-zoning scheme reached κ 0.71 with seven categories and a 111-page codebook, and CoreSC reached 0.50–0.57 with eleven; this taxonomy has fifteen labels.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

2. Fifteen fine labels each map to exactly one coarse type, and the mapping is a lookup table rather than a judgment. Four labels are anchored on categories with published inter-annotator figures -- `observation` on CoreSC Observation (κ 0.79), `procedure` on CoreSC Method (0.74), `definition` on CoreSC Object (0.81), `background` on CoreSC Background (0.87) -- and the remaining eleven are [DESIGN] with no anchor.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

3. The κ figures attached to the fine labels are the measured agreement for the *anchor* category in the cited scheme, not for the label as written in this specification. The CoreSC figures come from Liakata et al., LREC 2010 -- per-category one-vs-rest Cohen's κ over 41 chemistry and biochemistry papers with expert annotators.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

4. `general` is assigned by code alone: it is never offered as a label, never named in the prompt, and has no definition the model can see. [VERIFIED] A named fallback given to the model is catastrophic -- four frontier models given one plus the instruction to assign it for unknown cases recorded 96.1% agreement with Fleiss κ −0.001 and identified the minority class zero times, over 58,000-plus comments in MultiSoc-4D.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

5. [MEASURED] Self-reported confidence does not work as the trigger for `general`, tested directly: raters scored all fifteen labels 0–100 and code assigned `general` when no label cleared a bar. Picking one label directly reached α 0.934 and argmax over the scores reached 0.930, but every abstention rule lost -- margin ≥ 5 gave 0.893, absolute threshold 75 gave 0.892, threshold 90 collapsed to 0.605 with 86% of assignments falling through, and threshold 95 to 0.140 with 99%.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

6. [MEASURED] The reason confidence-based abstention fails is not miscalibration: raters disagree about their own uncertainty more than they disagree about the label. Eight raters can all pick `procedure` and score it 95, 88, 72, 91, 60, 85, 78 and 93, so under any threshold some abstain and some do not -- a statement they unanimously agreed on becomes a disagreement between `procedure` and `general`. Abstention does not filter noise, it manufactures it.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

7. [MEASURED] Four labels were removed after the first collision test. `conclusion` merged into `finding` because they were the worst-colliding pair in the set (11 co-occurrences, α 0.607 and 0.680) and the measured-versus-inferred distinction did not survive contact with real statements. `study`, `permission` and `tradeoff` were dropped as not carrying their weight -- though all three also drew zero assignments through an item-set coverage gap, so the test did not independently show them weak and the decision is editorial.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

8. [MEASURED] The status ladder reaches α 0.896 on 160 fresh statements from eight sources with four blind raters -- higher than the type taxonomy scores on the same items (0.877). Per rung: `floated` 0.938, `settled` 0.914, `evidenced` 0.906, `proposed` 0.851.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

9. [MEASURED] `floated` and `proposed` separate cleanly with only 2 disagreeing rater-pairs, the least confused pair in the ladder -- and this was the boundary predicted to be weak, on the reasoning that both are hedged and differ only by degree of specification. The prediction was wrong. Merging them changes α by +0.001, so the fourth rung costs nothing and buys resolution; the real trouble is at the top, with `evidenced`/`settled` at 21 and `evidenced`/`proposed` at 20.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

10. [MEASURED] Asking for status does not cost type agreement, it improves it. On the same 160 items, raters asked for type alone reached fine α 0.841 while raters asked for type and status reached 0.877, with coarse moving 0.840 to 0.896. This was the main risk in the two-field design and it inverted. The unproven mechanism offered is that with nowhere to record how established a statement is, raters were folding that judgment into the type choice.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

11. [MEASURED] The independent half of status is concentrated on particular types, measured as residual entropy of status given type: `dependency` 1.84 bits, `procedure` 1.74, `recommendation` 1.73, `principle` 1.72, down to `observation` 0.19 (97% evidenced, near-redundant) and `definition` 0.00 (100% settled, carrying nothing). Status earns its place on proposals, approaches and causal claims; on `definition` it is a constant. That argues for scoping the field rather than dropping it, and v1 has not acted on it.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

12. [MEASURED] Raters do not want an `n/a` status: 9 of 640 assignments, 1.4%. Status applies to almost every statement raters saw, including rules and events. The one exception is `general`, where half the assignments were `n/a` -- consistent with `general` being a residual rather than a kind.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

13. [MEASURED] A known gap is worse than predicted: a measured result that drives nothing -- 'the signal earned 0.82 Sharpe net of costs over the full sample' -- has no obvious home since `claim`/`finding` was removed. This was expected to fall to `general` and the control run shows it does not: raters reached for the escape hatch 0 times of 288, and instead split across 37 disagreeing rater-pairs between `observation` and `principle`, the largest collision measured in any run. So `general`'s share is not the metric that surfaces this -- fine-tier α on a results-dense corpus is.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

14. [MEASURED] The headline improvement from the codebook restructure was confounded, and a control shows the gain was not the taxonomy. The first and second tests used different item sets, so re-running the first test's 72 items through the second codebook isolates the taxonomy: the headline reported +0.080 fine and +0.061 coarse, but the taxonomy alone moved +0.009 fine and **−0.075** coarse, while the item set alone accounted for +0.071 and +0.136.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

15. [MEASURED] The third collision test measured the renames and the strip test on both earlier item sets at once -- 152 statements, four blind raters, two arms differing only in whether the strip test was present -- and improved fine α by +0.096 on the 72 results-dense items (0.787 to 0.883) and +0.072 on the 80 mixed items (0.858 to 0.930), with the item set held fixed in each pair. Two renames account for most of it: `driver` to `principle` moved α 0.623 to 0.910, and `structure` to `architecture` moved 0.727 to 0.851.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

16. [MEASURED] The strip test works locally and vanishes in aggregate: on the `principle`/`observation` boundary it targets, disagreeing rater-pairs fell from 37 under the control to 17 once pointers and names were fixed and to 11 with the strip test -- a further 35% -- while its effect on aggregate α is ±0.012, which four raters cannot distinguish from noise. It is retained because the boundary it fixes is cross-coarse, and the coarse tier is the one consumers read.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

17. [MEASURED] A generality test written for `principle` -- requiring that a causal claim still apply to the next case -- was tested as a fourth arm and rejected. It resolved five of the six items it was written for and broke five that had been unanimous: `principle`/`observation` collisions rose from 17 to 19, `principle` α fell from 0.861 to 0.797, and 51 assignments migrated from `principle` to `observation`. It cut both ways, which is the tell.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

18. [MEASURED] The generality test's failure confirms the §2.3 prediction on this codebook, on the label §2.3 named: a criterion that cannot be checked against the surface of the sentence introduces a judgment, and the judgment does not converge -- even when the criterion is correct in every individual case. The causal-diagnosis gap it was written to close is real and remains open.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

19. [VERIFIED] The definitions section, not the label count, determines whether the classifier works. Codebook depth and annotator training moved κ by .15–.36 on identical documents with an identical label set -- trained coders with a 17-page codebook, a decision tree and four training papers reached κ .65/.85/.87 where untrained coders given one page reached .35/.49/.72 -- while growing the label set from 3 to 7 cost only .07 (Teufel & Moens, EACL 1999).

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

20. [VERIFIED] Codebook depth is necessary but not sufficient: a 45-page codebook with a decision tree, category semantics, pairwise-distinction rules and worked examples still yielded only κ 0.50–0.57 in CoreSC.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

21. [DESIGN] The exemplars are drawn from quantitative finance and LLM/ML research because those are the domains the classifier runs on, but the anchor categories in §2.2 were measured on chemistry and computational-linguistics papers -- so the κ figures transfer to these definitions only as far as the category shapes do. This is recorded as a weakness in the evidence base rather than something the exemplars fix.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

22. [VERIFIED] Pairwise distinction rules are the bulk of a working codebook rather than a garnish: the scheme that reached κ 0.71 shipped 75 of them alongside a decision tree, in 111 pages of guidelines. [DESIGN] This specification carries nineteen rules covering the pairs judged to genuinely collide; pairs not listed were judged non-colliding, and a pair that turns out to collide in practice should be added to that section rather than patched into a definition.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

23. The `principle`/`observation` separation is resolved by the strip test: delete the numbers and the sample from the sentence, and if nothing of substance survives it is an `observation` -- even if the author generalizes from it. If a causal claim stands on its own without any measurement, it is a `principle`. [MEASURED] This is the largest collision measured in any run: 37 disagreeing rater-pairs in the control, 17 after repair and renaming, 11 with the strip test.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

24. The put-forward rule overrides content: anything proposed is a `recommendation`, whatever it proposes -- markers include we propose, proposal:, this RFC proposes, we recommend, is preferable, should, worth, what if, try, suggest. A fully specified system architecture that is being put forward is a `recommendation`, not an `architecture`. [MEASURED] 15 rater-pairs on proposed architectures, and the dominant signal in the 72-pair `procedure` collision.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

25. The `decision`/`procedure` separation asks whether the sentence names what was chosen instead of something else, with surface cues rather than, not X but Y, instead of, and we standardised on. Naming the rejected alternative makes it a `decision`; stating only how the thing is done makes it a `procedure`. [MEASURED] 7 disagreeing rater-pairs in the third test, across two coarse types.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

26. The `principle`/`recommendation` separation asks whether the statement is theoretical or hands-on: a `principle` is a general logical guide that would hold for anyone, stated at concept level, while a `recommendation` is practical advice drawn from experience bearing on a choice at hand where more than one valid option exists, and usually sounds conversational. [MEASURED] 18 rater-pairs.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

27. [MEASURED] The `obligation`/`prohibition` separation judges by the action demanded rather than grammatical polarity -- a negatively-phrased requirement that demands an action is an `obligation`. Both items in the 21-pair collision were compound, carrying a requirement and a forbidding in one sentence; under the resolution rules both tests score above 90 and the statement resolves to `general` with `multi_fire`.

   *backed by* `asmt-0001`, `asmt-0002`, `asmt-0003`

28. [MEASURED] The inference from binary framing to this label set has been tested directly and does not hold: single choice of fifteen reached fine α 0.877 and coarse 0.896, while the boolean battery resolved by priority order reached 0.785 and 0.805. Per-test agreement is good -- eleven of fifteen tests exceed α 0.87 -- and the loss is entirely in resolution: raters fire slightly different sets, and priority turns a small difference in the set into a different label. On 27 of 160 items all four raters fired a label in common and still resolved differently.

## 6. Statement classifier: contract, scope and evidence conventions

`create` · knowledge state **authoritative** · status `ready` · candidate `cand-006-r1` v2

**Slug** `statement-classifier-contract-scope-and-evidence-conventions`

### Summary

What the classifier promises, what it refuses to do, and how every claim in its specification is evidenced.

### Assertions (13)

1. The classifier's contract is one statement in, one classification record out, with an optional context argument. The context is used to classify that statement -- a line is often only readable against what came before -- but never to reclassify the neighbours themselves. [DESIGN] This reverses an earlier restriction that limited context to pronoun and ellipsis resolution, because several statements measured as unclassifiable in isolation are unambiguous in place.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

2. [MEASURED] Three separate in-house results form one pattern: confidence-based abstention lost, booleans plus priority resolution lost 0.092, and interior tiers lost 0.11. Anything that turns one classification decision into two costs more in the second step than it gains in the first.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

3. [MEASURED] Status is dependent on type but does not collapse into it: Cramér's V = 0.595 with mutual information 0.845 of 1.721 bits, so 49% of status is predictable from type and 51% is not. This test was run because the published two-axis design the spec cites failed when its axes turned out statistically dependent and collapsed into a few cells.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

4. [VERIFIED] Definitions must be written as surface tests rather than judgment calls: concretely-described features reached F1 above 0.60 while features requiring interpretive inference fell below 0.30, and model difficulty tracked human inter-coder difficulty at r = 0.61 -- so human disagreement is the practical ceiling. Measured over 7 models, 121 features and 567 excerpts.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

5. Every one of the fifteen definitions carries the same three-part shape: a **Cue** stating the surface pattern so a reader can check it without inferring intent, an **Excludes** list with at least two explicit non-firing conditions, and **Exemplars** giving one document-style statement and one conversational. Pairwise separations live in a single section rather than inside each definition, so a boundary is stated once and cannot drift between two entries.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

6. `formula` (coarse `model`) fires on something mathematical or scientific in nature -- an equation, an arithmetic definition in prose, or a statement defining something in mathematical or scientific terms. A `formula` says what a quantity equals and instructs nobody. The test is whether the arithmetic is the payload or machinery inside a policy.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

7. [VERIFIED] Presenting options as A/B/C/D inherits selection bias, and most of the effect comes from the option-ID tokens rather than position: removing the IDs cut recall standard deviation from 5.5 to 1.0 on MMLU, shuffling does not fix it, and a debiasing instruction barely helps (Zheng et al., 20 models). Tests are therefore emitted as named boolean fields -- no lettered options and no ordered menu.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

8. `modality` takes required, permitted or prohibited and is populated whenever a deontic modal is present, independent of which type test fired. It is validated only on statements that resolved to `rule` and otherwise retained but not enforced. [DESIGN] There is no measured evidence for it: deontic modals are surface cues, which is the property associated with reliable categories, but this specific field has not been evaluated.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

## 7. Statement classifier: the gaps its designers left open

`create` · knowledge state **preliminary** · status `ready` · candidate `cand-007-r1` v2

**Slug** `statement-classifier-the-gaps-its-designers-left-open`

### Summary

Named, unclosed gaps: content with no home in the taxonomy, work deliberately out of scope for v1, and questions where the search for evidence returned nothing that survived verification.

### Assertions (5)

1. The classifier answers 'what kind of statement is this' and never 'is this correct'. Truth judgment is explicitly out of scope, and epistemic status lives in a separate field rather than in the type.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

2. Permission-shaped statements have no home in the taxonomy. A sentence such as 'Analysts may exceed the intraday limit provided the book is flat at close' carries a deontic modal but is neither required nor prohibited, so it will land in `general` or be pulled into `obligation` by the modal. The specification directs watching `general`'s share on permission-heavy corpora such as policy documents.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

3. No evidence was found on three questions the design needs answered: resolving pronouns and ellipsis before classification, splitting one message into several statements, and how much surrounding context a conversational statement needs. Three separate research angles returned nothing that survived verification, and the only context datapoint anywhere in the evidence base is a 20–30 line window used as an unablated design choice.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

4. [DESIGN] The consequences for conversational input are that it may carry an optional context window used only for reference resolution, a statement whose references cannot be resolved is classified `general` rather than guessed, splitting a multi-statement message is out of scope for v1 with the caller supplying one statement, and conversational accuracy is expected materially below document accuracy and must be measured separately.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`

5. The classifier produces no truth judgment and no relationships between statements. Epistemic maturity is produced -- that is `status` -- but whether a statement is correct is not, and neither are edges between statements. Relations such as resolved_by and opposes are named as the natural next layer and are deliberately out of scope for v1.

   *backed by* `asmt-0004`
